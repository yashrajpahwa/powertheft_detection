###############################################################################
PART A - PRESENTATION SUMMARY (one block per slide)
###############################################################################

SLIDE 1 - TITLE
  TinyML Electricity-Theft Detection
  A 64 KB int8 TensorFlow Lite model that flags power theft on cheap edge
  devices with 97.9% accuracy
  Dataset: Mendeley Data - https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/c3c7329tjj/1

SLIDE 2 - PROBLEM
  - Electricity theft (meter tampering, bypassing, replaying readings) causes
    large revenue losses for utilities.
  - Smart meters produce hourly data, but sending everything to the cloud for
    analysis is costly and slow.
  - Goal: detect theft ON THE EDGE, on a microcontroller-class device
    (target: ESP32-S3, ~$5), with the best possible accuracy.

SLIDE 3 - OBJECTIVES
  - 7-class classification: Normal + 6 theft types.
  - Maximize accuracy on consumers the model has never seen.
  - Model under 100 KB, int8 quantized, TensorFlow Lite (TinyML).
  - Python-only pipeline, tested on a PC with the TFLite interpreter.

SLIDE 4 - DATASET
  - Source: Mendeley Data (link above), file df.csv, 560,655 hourly records.
  - 16 building types x 4 consumers = 64 consumers, 1 year each (8,760 h).
  - 10 hourly channels: 6 electricity sub-meters + 4 gas sub-meters (kW).
  - Labels: Normal 59.2%, Theft1 9.1%, Theft2 4.1%, Theft3 7.9%,
    Theft4 7.4%, Theft5 6.0%, Theft6 6.3%.
  - Cleaning: 15 all-zero junk rows removed -> exact 64 x 365 x 24 x 10 tensor.

SLIDE 5 - KEY DISCOVERY: THEFT IS A DAY-LEVEL PATTERN
  Analysis showed that theft is injected per DAY, and each type has a
  signature that is only visible against the consumer's past:
    T1  whole day x 0.1-0.8            -> sudden drop vs. history
    T2  zeros over a random interval   -> run of zero hours
    T3  each hour x random 0.1-0.8     -> jagged version of normal shape
    T4  random fractions of the mean   -> normal shape destroyed
    T5  24 h rolling average           -> unnaturally smooth curve
    T6  exact copy of yesterday (88%)  -> today == yesterday (replay)
  -> A single hour can't reveal these. The model must compare TODAY
     with YESTERDAY and a 7-DAY HISTORY.

SLIDE 6 - EVALUATION PROTOCOL (NO DATA LEAKAGE)
  - Split by CONSUMER: a consumer is never in both training and testing.
  - 4 folds x 16 consumers, one of each building type per fold.
  - Metrics: 7-class accuracy, macro-F1, per-class precision/recall,
    confusion matrix, theft-vs-normal accuracy, false-alarm rate.
  - Final test: fold 3 = 16 unseen consumers, 5,824 days.

SLIDE 7 - FEATURE ENGINEERING
  For every channel and day: today, yesterday, 7-day history mean.
  - Hourly curves (24 x 40): today, yesterday, history, |today-yesterday|,
    all divided by the consumer's normal level, so the model works for any
    building size.
  - Level (10): log of the normal level per channel.
  - 18 daily statistics x 10 channels = 180: ratio vs. history, zero hours,
    longest zero run, roughness, difference from yesterday, correlation with
    yesterday and with history, variability, peaks, jumps.

SLIDE 8 - MODELS TRIED (journey)
  1. Hour-by-hour Random Forest ................... 81.08%
  2. Gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM), day stats . 98.74% (too big: 965 KB)
  3. CNN on hourly curves only .................... 96.81%
  4. MLP on daily statistics only ................. 97.51%
  5. Hybrid CNN + statistics (17.5k params) ....... 97.99%
  6. Hybrid CNN + statistics (37.2k params) ....... 98.15%  <- SELECTED
  7. Selected model, int8 TFLite ................. 97.89% on unseen consumers

SLIDE 9 - FEATURE / CHANNEL ABLATION
  Which meter channels give the best accuracy (LightGBM, 4-fold CV):
    All 10 channels (elec + gas) .... 98.74%   <- chosen
    6 electricity channels .......... 98.45%
    Total kW only ................... 96.77%
  Adding yesterday's statistics as extra inputs: no gain (98.65%).

SLIDE 10 - FINAL MODEL ARCHITECTURE (Hybrid 1D-CNN, 37,216 params)
  Hourly curves (24x40) -> Conv1D 48 -> 3 residual dilated separable-conv
  blocks (dilation 2, 4, 8) -> average + max pooling (96)
  Statistics (180) -> built-in normalization
  Level (10)
  -> concatenate (286) -> Dense 64 -> Dense 64 -> Softmax (7 classes)
  Training: class-weighted cross-entropy, Adam 0.003 with cosine decay,
  60 epochs, batch 128.

SLIDE 11 - QUANTIZATION (float32 -> int8)
  Post-training full-integer quantization, 1,000 calibration days.
    Model            Accuracy   Size       Latency (PC)
    TFLite float32   97.94%     157.4 KB   18 us
    TFLite int8      97.89%      64.0 KB   37 us
  -> 2.5x smaller for a 0.05 point accuracy cost. 99.57% of predictions
     identical to the float model. All ops are TFLite-Micro compatible.

SLIDE 12 - RESULTS ON UNSEEN CONSUMERS (int8)
  Accuracy 97.89% | macro-F1 0.970 | theft-vs-normal accuracy 98.88%
  Per-class F1: Normal 0.990, T1 0.945, T2 0.956, T3 0.980, T4 0.987,
                T5 0.980, T6 0.948
  (Confusion matrix in Part B, table B5.)

SLIDE 13 - SYNTHETIC-DATA STRESS TEST (int8)
  Fresh thefts injected into unseen consumers, 17,472 days per scenario:
    Realistic thefts ......................... 98.68%
    New consumers (x0.2-5 scale, 5% noise) ... 98.25%
    Unseen variants (flat T5, reversed T6) ... 92.27%
  Theft detection rate 99%+, false alarms 0.7-1.7%.
  Blind spot: a time-REVERSED day is not detected (never seen in training).

SLIDE 14 - LIMITATIONS
  - Simulated consumption and synthetic thefts. Real meters are messier.
  - Needs at least 1 day (ideally 7 days) of history.
  - Unseen attack patterns (e.g. reversed day) can be missed.
  - Feature computation runs in Python before the model. A device would need
    the same preprocessing.

SLIDE 15 - FUTURE WORK
  - Train with augmented attack variants (reversal, flat days, partial days).
  - Distill the large tree model (98.74%) into the tiny CNN.
  - Quantization-aware training. Tune the alarm threshold.
  - Deploy on an ESP32-S3 and test with real smart-meter data.

SLIDE 16 - CONCLUSION
  - Day-level context (today vs. yesterday vs. 7-day history) is the key:
    81% (hour-level) -> 98% (day-level).
  - Final int8 TinyML model: 64 KB, 37 us per inference, 97.89% 7-class
    accuracy and 98.88% theft-vs-normal accuracy on never-seen consumers.
  - Robust to building size and noise (98.25% on rescaled synthetic data).

PART C - DETAILED METHODOLOGY
###############################################################################

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. OBJECTIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Build an electricity-theft detection model that is:
  - as accurate as possible at telling normal consumption apart from 6 types
    of theft (7-class classification), and
  - small and simple enough for inexpensive edge hardware (TinyML): a
    quantized int8 TensorFlow Lite model of about 100 KB or less, nominally
    targeting the ESP32-S3 class of microcontrollers.

Constraints set during the project:
  - Python and TinyML (TensorFlow Lite) only; quantization applied.
  - Testing is done on a PC with the TFLite interpreter. No firmware build.
  - Input features: whichever set gives the highest accuracy.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DATASET
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Source: Mendeley Data, "Theft detection in smart grid environment"
        https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/c3c7329tjj/1
File  : df.csv (64.4 MB)

The underlying consumption is simulated hourly energy use of commercial and
residential building types (originally from the Open Energy Data Initiative).
Theft examples were injected into it by a theft generator.

Columns (13):
  [0]      row index
  [1-6]    electricity sub-meters, kW (hourly):
             Electricity:Facility, Fans, Cooling, Heating, InteriorLights,
             InteriorEquipment
  [7-10]   gas sub-meters, kW (hourly):
             Gas:Facility, Heating:Gas, InteriorEquipment:Gas,
             Water Heater:WaterSystems:Gas
  [11]     Class: building type (16 types: FullServiceRestaurant, Hospital,
           LargeHotel, LargeOffice, MediumOffice, MidriseApartment, OutPatient,
           PrimarySchool, QuickServiceRestaurant, SecondarySchool, SmallHotel,
           SmallOffice, Stand-aloneRetail, StripMall, SuperMarket, Warehouse)
  [12]     theft: label, one of Normal, Theft1 ... Theft6

Rows: 560,655 hourly records.

Hourly label distribution:
  Normal  331,824  (59.2%)
  Theft1   51,083  ( 9.1%)
  Theft2   22,958  ( 4.1%)
  Theft3   44,349  ( 7.9%)
  Theft4   41,460  ( 7.4%)
  Theft5   33,553  ( 6.0%)
  Theft6   35,413  ( 6.3%)


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS AND KEY FINDINGS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Structure
  - The file is a sequence of 64 consecutive blocks of exactly 8,760 rows
    (= 365 days x 24 hours). Each block is one consumer-year.
  - 16 building types x 4 consumers each = 64 consumers.
  - 15 junk rows (all values 0, Class = "0") sit between blocks, at rows
    201480-201488 and 411729-411734. They were removed. After removal the
    data reshapes exactly into (64 consumers, 365 days, 24 hours, 10 channels).

3.2 Theft is injected per day
  - Labels change in runs of 24 hours, aligned to calendar days (9,088 runs of
    exactly 24 h). So the natural unit of classification is the DAY, not the hour.
  - Theft2 labels only the hours that were zeroed, so 1,967 days contain mixed
    hourly labels.

3.3 Reverse-engineering the actual theft types
  Each theft type was checked against the data by comparing tampered days with
  the neighbouring normal days:

  Theft1  The whole day is multiplied by one factor a, roughly U(0.1, 0.8).
          Measured factor vs. a normal day 7 days earlier: median 0.46,
          10th-90th percentile 0.18-0.75.
  Theft2  All channels set to 0 over a random interval of hours.
          Zeroed hours per day: median 13, interquartile range 9-16.
  Theft3  Each hour multiplied by its own random factor, about U(0.1, 0.8).
          The same factor applies to all 10 channels in that hour (correlation of
          per-hour ratios between channels = 1.00). The day keeps its shape but
          becomes jagged.
  Theft4  Each hour replaced by a random fraction of the day's mean. The normal
          daily shape is destroyed. The factors are shared across channels
          (correlation 0.96).
  Theft5  The day is replaced by a trailing 24-hour rolling mean of the
          original stream. The result is an unnaturally smooth curve. Hour 0
          equals yesterday's mean (median ratio 1.000).
  Theft6  An exact copy of the PREVIOUS day (a replay attack). 88.2% of Theft6
          days are bit-identical to the day before. Most of the rest are copies
          of the previous day taken before that day was itself tampered with.

  Consequences:
  - Several theft types (T1, T5, T6 especially) cannot be recognized from a
    single hour or even a single day on its own. They need context: yesterday
    and the consumer's recent history.
  - The gas channels are modified by the theft generator too (T1 reduced gas by
    the same factor), so they don't give the model an untampered reference.
    Using them is legitimate.

3.4 Day-level label distribution (64 x 365 = 23,360 days)
  Normal 13,139 | T1 2,137 | T2 1,645 | T3 1,839 | T4 1,731 | T5 1,398 | T6 1,471


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. DATA PREPARATION   (src/data.py)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Read df.csv and drop the 15 junk rows.
  2. Reshape into a tensor X of shape (64, 365, 24, 10), float32.
  3. Hourly labels yh (64, 365, 24): Normal = 0, Theft1..6 = 1..6.
  4. Day label yd (64, 365): the most frequent theft label present in the day.
     If the day has no theft hours it is Normal (0).
  5. Building-type index per consumer.
  6. Cache everything to cache/days.npz for fast reloading.
  7. Day 0 of every consumer has no history and is excluded from training
     and evaluation, leaving 64 x 364 = 23,296 usable days.
  8. Sanity checks (python src/data.py --check): 64 consumers x 8,760 h,
     4 consumers per building type, no consumer shared between folds.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. EVALUATION PROTOCOL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Leak-free grouped cross-validation:
  - Splits are made by CONSUMER, never by row or by day. A consumer is
    either entirely in training or entirely in testing.
  - 4 folds of 16 consumers. Each fold holds exactly one consumer of every
    building type (stratified by building type, seed 0), so every building
    type is present in both training and testing.
  - Reported as mean +- standard deviation over the 4 folds.

Why: a random row-level split puts neighbouring hours of the same consumer in
both train and test, which inflates accuracy. Holding out whole consumers
measures how the model behaves on a NEW meter.

Final hold-out: fold 3 (16 consumers, 5,824 days) is used as the test set for
the exported TFLite models and for the synthetic tests.

Metrics:
  - 7-class accuracy
  - macro-averaged F1 (each class weighted equally, so rare thefts matter)
  - per-class precision and recall, confusion matrix
  - binary theft-vs-normal accuracy
  - theft-detection rate (theft days flagged as any theft) and false-alarm
    rate (normal days flagged as theft)


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. FEATURE ENGINEERING   (src/features.py, src/train_cnn.py)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For each day and each channel c, four reference signals are built:
  x = today's 24 hourly values
  p = yesterday's 24 hourly values
  h = per-hour average of the previous 7 days (fewer at the start of the year)
  s = mean of h over the day + 0.001  (the consumer's "normal level")

6.1 Daily statistics, 18 per channel (180 total for 10 channels)
  Name          Formula                                      Mainly detects
  ------------  -------------------------------------------  --------------
  log_mean      log(1 + mean x)                              load level
  ratio_prev    log(mean x / mean p)                         T1
  ratio_hist    log(mean x / mean h)                         T1
  cv            std x / mean x                               T5 (low)
  cv_hist       std h / mean h                               reference
  min_rel       min x / mean x                               T2, T5
  max_rel       max x / mean x                               T4, T5
  n_zero        number of hours with x <= 0.001              T2
  zero_run      longest consecutive run of zero hours        T2
  rough         mean |2nd difference of x| / mean x          T3, T4 (high),
                                                             T5 (low)
  rough_ratio   log(rough(x) / rough(h))                     T3, T4, T5
  diff_prev     max |x - p| / mean x                         T6 (= 0)
  corr_prev     Pearson correlation(x, p)                    T6 (= 1)
  corr_hist     Pearson correlation(x, h)                    T4 (low)
  corr_rev      Pearson correlation(reversed x, h)           reversal
  lr_mean       mean over hours of log(x / h)                T1
  lr_std        std over hours of log(x / h)                 T3
  step_max      max |x[t] - x[t-1]| / mean x                 T2, T3, T4
  (A constant of 0.001 is added inside every ratio and log to avoid
   division by zero.)

  For the neural network each statistic f is compressed with
      f' = sign(f) * log(1 + 100*|f|)
  This tames heavy tails while keeping exact zeros at exactly zero, which
  matters for diff_prev on replayed days.

6.2 Hourly profile input "seq" (24 hours x 40 values)
  For each channel: x/s, p/s, h/s, |x - p|/s, clipped to [0, 8].
  Dividing by s removes the consumer's scale (a hospital and a shop become
  comparable). The |x - p| curve is all zero for a replayed day.

6.3 Level input "lvl" (10 values)
  log(1 + s) per channel. This gives back the absolute size of the load that
  the scaling removed.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. MODELLING EXPERIMENTS (chronological)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All results use the grouped 4-fold protocol of section 5 unless marked
"fold 0", which means a quick single-fold trial.

7.1 Hour-level baseline   (src/train_baseline.py)
  Random Forest, 100 trees, input = one hourly row (10 channels + building
  type), target = hourly label.
    Accuracy 81.08% +- 1.77     macro-F1 0.640
  This confirms that single hours don't carry enough information.

7.2 LightGBM on daily statistics   (src/train_gbm.py)
  Gradient-boosted trees, 400 rounds, 31 leaves, learning rate 0.05,
  subsample 0.8, colsample 0.8, balanced class weights.

  Channel-set ablation (to choose the input features):
    Channels used                    Accuracy          macro-F1
    all 10 (elec + gas)              98.74% +- 0.19    0.9795   <- best
    6 electricity                    98.45% +- 0.15    0.9753
    total facility kW only           96.77% +- 0.29    0.9515
  Adding yesterday's statistics as extra inputs ("lag") did not help:
    all 98.65%, electricity 98.34%, total 96.81%.
  -> Decision: use all 10 channels, no lag features.
  98.74% is the accuracy ceiling reached in this project, but the model is
  large (about 965 KB as a flat tree array).

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. FINAL TINYML MODEL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Inputs: seq (24 x 40), lvl (10), feat (180) as described in section 6.

Architecture (37,216 parameters):

  seq (24x40)
    -> Conv1D(48, kernel 3, same padding, no bias) -> BatchNorm -> ReLU
    -> 3 residual blocks, dilation 2, 4 and 8:
         SeparableConv1D(48, kernel 3, dilation d) -> BatchNorm
         -> Add(skip connection) -> ReLU
    -> GlobalAveragePooling1D (48)  +  GlobalMaxPooling1D (48)
  feat (180) -> Normalization layer (mean/std adapted on training data,
                stored inside the model)
  Concatenate [avg 48, max 48, lvl 10, feat 180] = 286
    -> Dense(64, ReLU) -> Dropout(0.2) -> Dense(64, ReLU)
    -> Dense(7, Softmax)

Design rationale:
  - The dilated convolutions (receptive field covers the whole 24 h) learn
    shape patterns: smoothness, jaggedness, zero intervals, similarity of
    today to yesterday or history.
  - Separable convolutions keep the arithmetic and parameter count low.
  - The engineered statistics give exact quantities (for example "difference
    from yesterday = 0") that a small CNN finds hard to compute on its own.
  - Average plus max pooling captures both typical and peak pattern strength.

Training:
  - Loss: sparse categorical cross-entropy.
  - Class imbalance: per-sample weights = N / (7 x count of that class).
  - Optimizer: Adam, initial learning rate 0.003, cosine decay to 0.
  - 60 epochs, batch size 128, fixed random seed.
  - No early stopping on test data. The schedule is fixed in advance.
  - Training ran on CPU, about 1-3 minutes per fold.

9. QUANTIZATION TO INT8 TENSORFLOW LITE   (src/export_tflite.py)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Procedure:
  1. Train the final model on folds 0-2 (48 consumers).
  2. Convert to float32 TFLite (reference).
  3. Post-training full-integer quantization:
       - optimizations = DEFAULT
       - representative dataset = 1,000 random training days (calibrates the
         value range of every tensor)
       - supported ops restricted to TFLITE_BUILTINS_INT8
       - input and output types int8
     All weights and activations are int8.
  4. Evaluate both models with the TFLite interpreter on fold 3 (16 unseen
     consumers, 5,824 days).

Quantization parameters (real value = scale x (q - zero_point)):
  seq     scale 0.031373    zero_point -128   (range 0 .. 8)
  feat    scale 0.099454    zero_point  -56
  lvl     scale 0.142871    zero_point  -26
  output  scale 0.00390625  zero_point -128   (probabilities 0 .. 1)

TFLite operators: CONV_2D, DEPTHWISE_CONV_2D, FULLY_CONNECTED, ADD, MEAN,
REDUCE_MAX, CONCATENATION, SOFTMAX, SUB, MUL, RESHAPE, EXPAND_DIMS,
SPACE_TO_BATCH_ND, BATCH_TO_SPACE_ND. All are standard builtins supported
by TensorFlow Lite Micro.

Quantization outcome (fold 3):
  Model            Accuracy   macro-F1   Size       Latency (PC CPU)
  Keras float32    97.94%     -          -          -
  TFLite float32   97.94%     0.9697     157.4 KB   18 us / day
  TFLite int8      97.89%     0.9695      64.0 KB   37 us / day
  int8 predictions agree with the float model on 99.57% of days.
  -> 2.5x smaller for a 0.05 percentage-point accuracy cost.

11. SYNTHETIC-DATA TESTING OF THE INT8 MODEL   (src/synth_test.py)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: check that the int8 model generalizes to freshly generated theft that
it has never seen, on consumers it has never seen.

Method:
  1. Take the 16 held-out consumers (fold 3).
  2. Build a clean year for each: every non-normal day is replaced by the
     nearest fully normal day of the same consumer (same weekday preferred).
  3. Inject new thefts with our own generator, independently for each day with
     probability 0.4 and a theft type chosen uniformly from 1-6. Thefts are
     applied in sequence, so the history can itself contain earlier thefts,
     as in the real data.
       T1  day x U(0.1, 0.8)
       T2  zero from a random start hour to a random end hour
       T3  each hour x U(0.1, 0.8), shared by all channels
       T4  channel mean x U(0.1, 0.8) x U(0, 1) per hour
       T5  trailing 24 h rolling mean of the untampered stream
       T6  copy of the previous (possibly tampered) day
  4. Three scenarios, each with 3 random seeds (17,472 days per scenario):
       realistic   the generator above
       new_cons    each consumer rescaled by a random factor in x0.2 .. x5,
                   plus 5% multiplicative hourly noise, then thefts injected
                   (simulates new, different-sized consumers and meter noise)
       unseen      variants never present in training: T5 = a perfectly
                   flat day mean, T6 = the day reversed in time
  5. Run the int8 TFLite model and score it.

    the real held-out data, because synthetic days have no overlapping or

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. LIMITATIONS AND POSSIBLE IMPROVEMENTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Limitations:
  - The consumption data is simulated and the thefts are synthetic. Real
    meters have outages, holidays and behaviour changes that could cause
    false alarms.
  - The model needs at least one previous day (ideally 7) of history.
  - Unseen theft patterns (for example a reversed day) may go undetected.
  - The engineered statistics are computed in Python before the TFLite model.
    On a microcontroller this preprocessing would need to be implemented
    alongside the model.
  - Uses all 10 sub-meter channels. A meter that only measures total kW would
    see lower accuracy (about 96.8% in the tree-model ablation).

Possible improvements:
  - Train with augmented theft variants (reversed days, flat days, partial-day
    attacks) to close the blind spots.
  - Knowledge distillation: train the CNN to imitate the large LightGBM model
    (98.74%) to narrow the gap of about 0.6 points.
  - Average a few small models trained with different seeds.
  - Quantization-aware training (the post-training loss is already tiny,
    0.05 points).
  - Tune a decision threshold on the "Normal" probability to trade false
    alarms against detections.
